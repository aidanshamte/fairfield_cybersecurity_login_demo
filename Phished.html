01 / The lesson

The most convincing attacks
often look ordinary.

The poster used familiar university styling, a QR code, and a tempting reward. These are common ingredients in social engineering: creating trust, urgency, or curiosity to encourage action.

▦

QR code phishing

A QR code hides its destination until you inspect the link. A professional-looking poster does not guarantee a safe website.

✉

Social engineering

Attackers may impersonate trusted organizations and offer rewards to persuade people to visit deceptive pages.

🛡

MFA is a defense

Multi-factor authentication adds protection, but fake sign-in pages and unexpected approval prompts still require attention.

Remember: a familiar logo is not proof of authenticity.

Check the full URL, avoid entering credentials through unexpected links, and never approve a Duo prompt you did not initiate.

04 / Your next move

Pause. Verify. Protect.

Inspect the destination. Before opening a QR link, check its URL preview and look for unexpected domains or misspellings.

Use trusted sign-in paths. Navigate to university services through known bookmarks or the official university portal rather than unfamiliar links.

Decline unexpected Duo requests. If you receive an MFA prompt you did not initiate, do not approve it. Contact ITS through official support channels.

Report suspicious messages. Alert the university's IT security/support team using a verified contact method.
